## Original premise and dramatic purpose

The recovered Bellwether diagram describes a northbound bond-and-deed consignment on the **Morrow Consolidated Line**. Rafe Calder meant to detach its custody coach on a quarry siding, substitute a warrant ledger, and sell already-paid homes a second time. Tomas proposes reaching the coach before Calder. Della insists on recovering evidence and securing the disputed property; Bastian calls it an opportunity to obtain the money the community needs to move. The plan becomes a robbery of a much larger enterprise than any of them has yet confronted.

**Octavia Morrow**, the line's industrial principal, owns controlling interests in rail transport, peat fuel, wharf warehousing, and timber concessions. She measures control through bonds, freight restrictions, debt assignments, and private investigators. Her private coach is present; she is away at a coast shareholders' meeting. A portrait, signed instructions, and incompatible versions of the account establish her influence without placing an invulnerable boss on the train. She is a separate continuing antagonist from Calder and Ansel Voss. Her full industrial, civic, financial, and later-conflict arc remains to be independently authored.

The community could leave the mountain without attacking the train. Tomas argues that departure without the deeds leaves their homes legally lost; Della asks whether securities taken under force can fund a lawful challenge. Mara is willing to recover the papers but rejects treating every employee as an extension of the owner. This disagreement lasts beyond acceptance and cannot be solved by selecting a single “good” briefing answer.

An electrical failure sends Mara and a new young companion onto a moving train while their carefully assigned party is separated. Success requires trusting that companion, protecting a real hanging body, fighting through articulated cars, stopping an actual engine, surviving a larger guard response, opening a fortified carriage, searching its interior, and deciding what happens to individual unarmed workers. The unsold securities create future opportunity and danger. Returning with them does not create immediate spendable wealth or complete the community's relocation.

## Original region, train, and accessible geometry

Author **`brass-cutting`**, a long thawing valley beneath an abandoned quarry viaduct. A working starting proposal is approximately 6,200 × 2,800 world units with a curved south-to-northeast main line, a low service siding, a roofed rock cutting, a stone maintenance shelf, a fir-lined bridle road, a water mast, a turnout hut, a drainage gallery, and a northern signal board. These dimensions and speeds are original, untested production values. The operation does not rename Bellwether's wagon traverser, Copperglass station, or the rescue's ice climb.

The plan uses a quarry charge to release the jammed lock on an unused turnout's obstruction board. Its raised red face should make the engineer brake before the narrow cutting. Ruth positions the charge on the visible service plate beside the line, away from an occupied coach; the intent is a signal stop, not an invisible explosion that safely immobilizes everyone. The line's physical danger remains clear. The recovered diversion diagram warns that the creek siding cannot take the locomotive, so the party must not blindly drive the whole train onto it.

Each car has a stable entity ID, length, coupler transform, roof/interior walkable surfaces, moving cover, windows, doors, steps and collision volumes.
