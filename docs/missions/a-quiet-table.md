# A Quiet Table — original hunting mission specification

Research and authoring date: 2026-10-08. Stable mission ID: `snowbound-a-quiet-table`. Source coverage requirement: `campaign-the-aftermath-of-genesis`. Destination: I — Snowbound. Status: original production authoring with runtime and visual integration in progress; source and whole-game acceptance remain open. Its three distinct optional conditions concern one-arrow kills, avoiding spooking, and a clean second kill. Other weapons are allowed; footprints can suffice without activating the new tracking sense. Failure includes protected companion/mount losses, irretrievable deer escape, and ruined carcasses. ## Original premise and participation

Orla Venn has turned the kiln's spare drying shed into a communal kitchen. Silas needs fresh broth, Gideon still struggles to eat, and the residents must prepare food for the eventual move east. The earlier recovered food remains in stock: Orla acknowledges a well-supplied pantry rather than deleting its contents to manufacture hunger. Preserved rations kept people alive; fresh meat extends the next few days and supports the patients.

Freight blasting has displaced the deer from their usual feeding ground. Juno Mercier knows where an unfrozen reed bank survives below Willow Run, but two crushed fingers prevent her from drawing a bow. She asks Mara to make the shots while she reads sign and brings home a second load. Mara doubts that an injured packer should leave the kiln. Juno answers by showing the route on a flour-dusted tabletop, pointing out its sheltered rests and the animals' feeding ground. Her knowledge and limits both matter physically.

- **Orla Venn:** former boardinghouse cook for night-shift rail workers, now organizing shared meals. Her humor comes from arguing about portions and who washed the pot; her biography and lines are original. She teaches processing and community contribution, with an actual pantry ledger.
- **Juno Mercier:** Métis packer and tracker from a Cree- and French-speaking trapping family. A freight brake crushed two fingers during the eviction. She can ride, inspect sign, and support a load on her healthy shoulder, but cannot draw a bow or perform the skinning lesson. Her later discussion of belonging concerns being paid fairly for skilled work and having a say in the residents' plans. Further cultural/character research and original visual design remain required; this outline is not character acceptance.
- **Bracken:** Juno's dark bay pack horse with one white fetlock. Ownership, temperament, health, tack, and large-load capacity are distinct from Copper. Bracken must be an actual persistent mount, not an interchangeable follower sprite.
- **Moss Laird and Vera Holl:** the two camp helpers have already returned from an unsuccessful attempt. They describe noise from a falling shovel and disagree about which track they followed. Neither is collapsed into a generic “failed hunter.”
- **Hob Jarrow:** a cobbler warming his boots in the kitchen. He tries to claim credit for choosing the route, then physically leaves when Orla asks him to clean the workbench. He has his own minor participation and later availability.
- **Della Wren:** records yields and explains the distinction between community food, a donated hide, and a hide retained for later trade. She cannot award imaginary sale proceeds.
- **Tomas, Silas, Gideon, Elin, Fin, Ada, and Inez:** preserve their existing identities and states. Patients remain patients; optional camp dialogue acknowledges the rescue and all earlier outcomes without replacing their roles with new quest markers.

Mara and Copper are the existing protagonist/mount. Juno chooses to join the residents after the expedition because they make a place for skilled work and shared decisions. This resolves a local relationship concern; it does not imitate an outlaw gang's recruitment history. No source dialogue, biography, costume, name, map, or dramatic scene is copied.

## Geography and objects

Add `willow-run`, a lower watershed reached by a visible branch from the north trail. It retains the kiln and north-cutting worlds, including the station damage, upper ice closure, dead Lark, Silas's care state, Copper's ownership/pack, and Pavel/Voss outcomes.

The route passes a thawing culvert, a noisy freight road, a sheltered dismount bank, a reed pool, a shallow creek crossing, a cedar feeding shelf, and a rock-sided bear gorge. The return follows a different hillside path to the kiln's drying shed. It must not rename the Copperglass combat yard or re-use the rescue climb as a deer clearing. Snow, mud, exposed grass, and water produce different readable sign. A sheltered rest fits both horses and a carrying adult.

Author two individual prey entities, provisionally `willow-creek-doe` and `willow-cedar-buck`, with separate life/wound state, poses, alert history, quality, tracks, and carcass identity. Their proposed species/sex variants need later compendium research; this proposal does not seal the source's exact animal variants. Track nodes must lead from the ground actually occupied by that deer. When prey relocates, newly authored or sampled tracks continue from its real route, rather than leaving a magical marker at the old position.

Place a cinnamon-colored bear across a narrow gorge with a rock shoulder preventing direct access during the staged encounter. It is visible, reacts, frightens the mounts, and moves into cover as the party detours. There is no reachable extra trophy carcass or kill objective. Prefer physical separation and retreat to an unexplained infinite-health actor. How the source's non-killable encounter behaves still needs observation; later free-roam bear hunting remains a separate requirement.

Copper and Bracken each have one rear large-carcass slot. This is independent of item pack capacity, a weapon rack, and a passenger seat. A whole deer occupies one slot; neither horse can secretly carry both. The drying shed contains a hitch, unloading space, two reachable processing surfaces, a knife/tool station, a hide rack, and the pantry. Camera cutaways must make the hands, load, tool, carcass, and destination readable.

## Teaching and recovery rules

The bow has independent ammunition from the revolver and coach gun. Drawing consumes stamina gradually; prolonged hold increases sway. Canceling the draw does not spend an arrow. Releasing emits one arrow with collision, travel, target-zone damage, and a recoverable/broken/spent outcome. Paused menus and dialogue cannot keep charging the draw or release an unintended arrow after resume. A bow is not an instantaneous nearest-enemy fire command.

Noise, line of sight, and wind influence alert state. Teach these with visible cues and short companion feedback before punishing a novice. Exact ranges, scent cones, wind strength, timers, and damage values are proposed balancing, not established source facts. Calm movement and a sheltered approach should allow a patient route. A missed arrow or brief spook opens recovery; it does not silently terminate the mission.

Wounded prey can move into another patch, leaving blood sign. Juno identifies the last useful sign without shooting the animals for the player. A humane finishing shot or reachable knife action can complete the hunt while forfeiting an incompatible optional condition. Deliberately leaving an injured deer to bleed out affects honor only from the actual wound/outcome history. A clean kill is an immediate vital hit with the appropriate weapon/impact, not a skinning animation or a final “deer collected” counter.

Alternate available weapons remain playable and prompt Juno's criticism. They fail the bow-specific optional predicate where applicable, but do not automatically fail the expedition. Gun noise can provoke a recoverable relocation; it must not always cause instant, unavoidable permanent escape. Exhausting all arrows is not an excuse to invent unlimited ammo. Existing weapons, recovered arrows, a humane finish, or a checkpoint provide clear recovery. Delayed bow retrieval leaves Juno in a robust waiting state with repeated readable assistance, avoiding the indexed freeze edge.

Return talk uses named facts from the actual campaign. If the later rival-camp mission has already occurred, mention its operation and its separately authored captive. Pavel must not serve as that captive: he can already be released or killed, and his opening fate remains permanent. Before the rival mission, Tomas can discuss the hide/workbench and the next search. The advanced combat-targeting lesson unlocks only from its proper mission; the opening's basic Focus is not silently reclassified as that lesson.

## Yields, ownership, and unlocks

The required pair is a community hunt: its meat reaches Orla. Hide quality affects a later trade value, independently from whether enough edible meat can be processed. Both usable carcasses support completion; low hide quality cannot cause an unrelated pantry softlock. As a provisional balance, each usable mission deer could produce six meal portions, but this number needs playtesting and is entirely original authoring. Record the actual result before any cooking, and deduct actual fuel/ingredients only when used. If the earlier kindling was exhausted during lawful play, the kitchen provides its own explicitly authored starter processing fuel; it does not consume a nonexistent item.

Each hide has an instance ID, quality, owner, and location. The player's retain/donate choice changes ownership and the rack record once. Explaining later sale/trade does not produce cash or claim that merchant functionality already exists. The second hide may belong to the community by Juno's decision, which is shown in dialogue and the account. A retained hide is removable through a real rack interaction after completion; a donated hide cannot also be sold by Mara.

The bow and its remaining arrows stay owned after the mission. Granting a field knife at the bench is an optional new authoring choice if the wider equipment plan has not already supplied one; use a single instance/grant and never replace an existing player knife. The ration remains consumed or retained according to actual use. Tracking and field-skinning skills unlock from taught actions. Camp contributions and weapon/rack usage continue into free roam with genuine ownership.

Availability requires the rescue's permanent completion. Hunting and the original rival-camp counterpart can be attempted in either order. The later train/heist counterpart requires both completions. Completing the second prerequisite can queue a voiced request or camp scene, but an unavailable, unauthored heist cannot become a fake playable button. Keep an opportunity to inspect the relevant camp interactions before transitioning; their full source window still needs audit.

## Optional performance and failure

Keep three separate predicates and display their actual state:

1. Each of the two named deer was killed by its sole arrow impact; extra impacts or another finishing method invalidate the relevant record. Record misses separately until exact source grading is verified.
2. Neither named deer ever entered spooked state during the attempt.
3. The second deer died from an immediate clean vital hit.

Do not replace these with total accuracy, a generic hunting score, or a two-body pickup count. Final exact source grading rules remain to be verified. For the original attempt, checkpoint retry can mark best-condition eligibility according to an explicit policy, while a full restart/replay resets it; this is a proposed policy, not a researched source assertion.

Readable failure reasons cover Mara's death; protected Juno/Copper/Bracken death or deliberate assault; sustained party/mount abandonment; an irretrievably escaped required animal; or destruction/loss of a required carcass. Nearby relocation, a recoverable wound, a wrong trail, and a missed arrow are recovery states. If carcass decay or destructive trampling is implemented, teach and warn before the limit, and derive failure from the actual entity. Shooting a delivered carcass must not mint a new corpse or yield. Tests must use real projectiles for protected-actor failures, not only altered hit-point fixtures.

## State, checkpoints, and replay

Reuse the incoming version-2 campaign graph rather than another stage-specific monolith. This paragraph proposes contracts and does not claim those APIs already exist.

- The mission record owns flags, choices, performance, tutorial states, transaction IDs, stage, and checkpoint references. Prior opening/rescue records and their historical choices remain unchanged.
- The entity registry owns both deer, Juno, Bracken, Mara, Copper, and body/load identity. Deer life, wound state, quality, alert history, and carcass-processing state have one authoritative representation. Regions store environment/residency, not duplicate bodies.
- A carcass has exactly one location: ground, carried by one actor, attached to one mount's large slot, on one bench, or processed. Attachment changes are atomic. Pack, passenger, and weapon-rack occupancy use separate typed slots and reject illegal combinations/cycles.
- Weapon instances own bow possession/location; arrow events use stable projectile/recovery IDs. Track/scent/noise samples and last confirmed clues survive save/load. A partially drawn bow either restores a validated state or safely cancels with no ammunition loss under a documented policy.
- Stable transactions cover ration gift, bow/arrow gift, optional knife gift, each arrow recovery, each carcass load/unload/delivery, each skinning yield, hide ownership change, pantry contribution, and cooking consumption. Reopening dialogue, loading, retrying, and replay cannot repeat them.

Checkpoint boundaries: accepted expedition before grants; equipment secured; trail confirmed before the first shot; first deer dead before crossing to the second; both carcasses safely loaded; safe hillside after the bear; both bodies delivered before processing. Use nonrecursive snapshots of the full campaign graph with separate checkpoint collections, consistent with the journey codec. Each captures all regions, actor/mount health and ownership, prey positions/wounds/alert/death, tracks, arrow drops/ammunition, equipment, carry/load/bench ownership, quality, yields, pantry/hide records, durable choices, local dialogue, and transaction history. A retry restores an earlier mission-local hunt legitimately without rewriting unrelated canonical branches or cloning a gift.

Version validation rejects duplicate deer IDs, invalid life/processed combinations, impossible future checkpoint progress, attachment cycles, duplicate loaded carcasses, mismatched ammo, invalid transaction references, and contradictory region residence. Migration from valid earlier saves adds an unstarted hunting record and its authored cast only when lawful; it cannot grant the bow, deer yields, completion, or a manufactured missing Juniper.

Replay selects the completed hunting ID and its recorded entry snapshot plus reviewed authored loadout. It gets local bow/ammo regardless of a later campaign's equipment choices. All grants, prey deaths, yields, hides, stock changes, and story choices remain isolated. Exiting successful, failed, or suspended replay restores the full canonical graph, existing checkpoints, player health, money, honor, wanted/bounty state, pack, dropped items, all regions, and every prior mission's durable state. A separate best-performance record may change only under an explicit policy. Replay must neither award a second bow nor resurrect canonical prey or overwrite permanent choices.
